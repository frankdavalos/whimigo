# Through the first release, art and sound are made in code

Until the end of Phase 3, everything a child sees and hears is authored in the repository by coding agents: art as SVG, sound as synthesis recipes. Nothing is recorded, illustrated by hand, commissioned or licensed, and there is no speech. The owner chose this to keep human involvement low while the Arcade is being built; hand-made art and recorded voice are possibilities for later expansion.

The trade-off is real. A human illustrator and recorded sound would be richer, and "beautiful and polished" is a stated goal. We accept a simpler look and sound in exchange for an Arcade that can be built, changed and extended without waiting on anyone's production time.

## Consequences

- The visual style has to be one that simple vector shapes do well: flat colour, bold outlines, rounded geometric forms, a limited palette. A written style guide is the first art task of Phase 1, and every Mascot and Game is drawn to it.
- The person's role is to look and listen, and to say what should change. It is not to produce.
- Instructions are shown by a Demo, because there is no voice ([ADR-0014](0014-synthesized-sound-no-voice.md)).
- Asset licensing is not a concern: there are no third-party assets.
- The Game contract leaves room for hand-made work later. A Game's pack can list image and audio files, so adding them is a content change and not an architectural one.
