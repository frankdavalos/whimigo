### Screen

| Item | Result |
|---|---|
| 12. Nothing hidden **(G5)** | Looks good: nothing a child must touch is hidden. |
| 13. Rotate to portrait and back | **Failed, then passed after a fix.** In the installed app (not in a Safari tab), turning from landscape to portrait made the picture zoom in, cutting off the controls in the corners; starting in portrait, and turning to landscape, were fine. Repeated on build `e14e3f5`: turning between landscape and portrait, either way and repeatedly, works. The rotate-to-landscape picture is out of scope in Phase 0; in portrait the landscape picture fits the width, with sky above and below. |
| 14. Windowed Apps mode | Usable. Turning from landscape to portrait had the same fault as item 13, and passes after the same fix. |
| 15. Sharpness **(P0)** | Looks great: the vector art, the bitmap digits and the sharpness card are sharp. |

**What was done about items 13 and 14.** Neither Chromium nor WebKit under Playwright showed the fault.

A first fix (`b5679b5`) guessed that iPadOS widened the page; it did not help. The status page was then given live screen readings (`b2c7786`), and in the installed app they showed the cause. The window's resize event fires while the Arcade's box is still landscape width. Phaser sized the canvas from the box then, and its own recheck, which runs from its frame loop, never caught up once the box took its portrait width. In portrait the window, page, visible area and box were all 1032 × 1344 at zoom 1; only the canvas stayed 1376 wide. In a Safari tab the box already has its new size when the event fires.

The fix (`e14e3f5`): the canvas follows the box whenever the box changes size, through a `ResizeObserver`, independent of the window's events and of Phaser's frame loop. A layout test reproduces the installed app's order of events.

### Performance (P0)

| Item | Result |
|---|---|
| 24. Work per frame, and the loop | Work per frame about **0.33 ms** under the standard load, against the 3 ms budget. Frames per second a rock-steady **60**. The add-and-remove loop counted to **50**. |
| 25. Five minutes in and out | No Games yet. Looks good. |
| 26. Low Power Mode | Still feels good. Work per frame about **0.65 ms**; frames per second a very stable **30**. |

**Note on item 24.** The iPad Pro's display can refresh at 120 Hz, and the overview's frame budget assumes 120 frames per second (8.3 ms a frame). The Arcade ran at 60. Safari, and web apps on the home screen, normally hold animation frames to 60 per second on such displays. Work per frame is well inside either budget. Whether 60 frames per second is acceptable is part of the gate decision.

## For the gate decisions

These are the readings that [roadmap.md](../roadmap.md#phase-0-technical-proof-of-concept) asks the run to supply. The decisions themselves are the owner's.

- **Highest touch count:** 10. The 11th touch is ignored.
- **Cancellations:** 0 with default settings, and 0 with the gesture toggles off and Guided Access on.
- **Work per frame:** 0.33 ms at 60 frames per second. In Low Power Mode, 0.65 ms at 30.
- **Sharpness:** sharp.
- **Sound:** returned after backgrounding, after locking and after reopening, and obeys silent mode.
- **Top of the screen:** usable; nothing hidden.
- **Crashes or wrong drawing:** no crashes. Wrong drawing after turning the installed app from landscape to portrait (items 13 and 14), fixed in `e14e3f5` and passing when repeated on the tablet.
